# QR codes: what it would take, and what it is for

A decision document, written against §"QR codes are a different project" in
[SYMBOLOGIES.md](SYMBOLOGIES.md). That section costs QR out and stops short of
a verdict, on the grounds that the verdict is a product question. This one
answers the product question, disagrees with three of the technical estimates,
and records the decisions taken. **Agreed and built** -- see the end of the
document for what was settled and by whom.

**Nothing here has been put in front of a scanner or a camera.** Everything is
arithmetic against `BarcodeLayout.hpp`, the QR Code capacity tables, and the
panel datasheet, plus two things measured with a script in this repository. The
places where a number is a guess are marked as guesses.

## The short version

1. **Build it, but not the QR the question implies.** QR as a second format for
   the *existing sixteen-character id*, opt-in per code. Not a longer payload,
   not a URL, not a second list.
2. **`kMaxIdLength` must not move.** Every id this app accepts today fits QR
   version 2 at error-correction level M with ten bytes to spare. Raising the
   limit is where the whole cost of this feature lives, and it buys the weakest
   use cases. Left alone, the message does not grow **by a single byte** —
   `Barcode::Code` already carries a `Format`.
3. **The encoder is cheaper than SYMBOLOGIES.md estimates**, because fixing the
   version and the EC level deletes most of QR: no version selection, no
   interleaving, no block structure table, no version-information modules, no
   mode selection. Measured at **+3,680 bytes on the package, 3.2 %**, of which
   the encoder is 2,856. See §What it costs -- including the 2 KB the first
   build wasted, and how the measurement found it.
4. **The screen is not the blocker, but the layout is more of one than that
   section allows.** A five-pixel module misses fitting by *one pixel row*. The
   achievable module is four pixels, 0.504 mm, and QR mode cannot show a code's
   name.
5. **For parkrun, QR is a regression, and that has to be said out loud.** The
   funnel scanner cannot read it. This is an opt-in format for the codes that
   are scanned by a camera, and `Format::Code128` stays the default and stays 0.

## Is it wanted, and for what?

SYMBOLOGIES.md says QR and Code 128 "are not substitutes". Agreed, and the
asymmetry runs one way only.

**The funnel scanner cannot read a matrix symbology at all.** parkrun's funnel
device is an Opticon OPN-2001, whose vendor material describes a "100 scans per
second **laser** engine" and which is listed throughout the trade as a 1D-only
device. A laser engine sweeps a line; there is no mechanism by which it reads a
grid. This is not a firmware limitation to be worked around.

**Virtual Volunteer's camera does read QR** — the app scans the Run Director's
Event QR Code to submit results, so QR decoding is in its pipeline. Whether its
*athlete scan* mode accepts a QR-encoded athlete id is **not confirmed from a
primary source**, and I could not reach parkrun's own pages
([support.parkrun.com](https://support.parkrun.com/hc/en-us/articles/200566883-1-4-Barcodes)
and the [Volunteer Hub](https://volunteer.parkrun.com/hc/en-us/articles/16857863282066-4-1-Virtual-Volunteer)
both return 403 to a fetch). The circumstantial case is decent: the App Store
version history records that "barcode scanning is stricter, only A or P followed
by numbers accepted", which is a rule about the *payload* and not the symbology;
Apple Wallet parkrun passes generated by dfyb.run are QR and are in widespread
use on Apple Watch at finish funnels; and
[the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2022/04/22/a-parkrun-barcode-on-your-garmin-or-apple-watch-parkrun-phone-apps/)
states flatly that "the parkrun scanning machines will also correctly scan a QR
code". That last claim cannot be true of the Opticon, which is the tell: what is
really being reported is that the *camera* route works.

So the parkrun answer is settled without needing the unverified bit:

| | laser funnel scanner | Virtual Volunteer camera |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Code 128 | reads it | reads it |
| QR | **cannot** | probably, unconfirmed |

**Against the laser, Code 128 is a superset of QR's usefulness.** Anyone whose
event scans with the Opticon and who switches to QR is strictly worse off, at
the one moment the app exists for.

**Against the ecosystem, that framing is too strong**, and this document said so
in an earlier draft. parkrun QR codes are a real and common thing: the Apple
Wallet passes generated by dfyb.run are QR, they sync to an Apple Watch, and
people are scanned from them at funnels every week. Whatever the Opticon can do,
the camera route is in routine use and it is what a watch gets scanned by.

So both are true, and the conclusion is the same either way: **QR is opt-in, per
code, and Code 128 stays the default.** A wearer who knows their event scans by
phone can pick QR; a wearer who does not, or who does not care, keeps the format
that both devices read.

### Then what is QR for?

Two real cases, and they are not the ones the question anticipates.

**The one that survives: density.** This is the argument SYMBOLOGIES.md does not
make, and it is the good one. A sixteen-character *alphanumeric* id is Code
128's worst case on this panel — 211 modules across 200 px, a 119 µm
X-dimension, 4.7 mil, under the 5 mil mid-density line that README.md uses as
its "any scanner should manage this" reference. Subset C rescued long *numeric*
ids and did nothing for that one. The same id as a QR symbol is a **504 µm**
module: four times the linear X-dimension, because a matrix symbology spends
area instead of width and this panel has area it is not using. For a gym card or
a library card with a long mixed id, scanned by a phone, QR is the better draw
by a wide margin.

**The one that does not: payload.** A URL, a ticket, a Wi-Fi credential, a
vCard. These are the things people mean when they say "QR", and every one of
them needs more than sixteen characters. §"Does the payload limit change?"
below prices that, and the answer is that it costs more than the feature is
worth. If someone wants a ticket on their wrist, that is a different app, and
SYMBOLOGIES.md's instinct — "two apps sharing a config file" — is right about
*that* app and wrong about this one.

So: **QR here is a second way to draw an id, not a way to carry a new kind of
value.** With that framing it is one app with a second format, and everything
below gets cheap.

## What the panel actually allows

Two measurements, both from a script over `BarcodeLayout::pixelIsLit`'s model
(a pixel is lit only if its centre is within 119.5 dot pitches):

- The largest square of lit pixels anywhere on the panel is **168 × 168**, at
  x = 36..203, y = 35..202. SYMBOLOGIES.md's "about 169 px" is right.
- That is not the number that matters. The id row starts at `kIdLine1Y = 169`,
  so a symbol that leaves the human-readable id alone must end by y = 168. The
  largest centred square with its bottom edge at y = 168 is **144 × 144**.

Against the sizes a fixed-version QR can be, at whole-pixel modules — and they
must be whole pixels, for the same four-grey-levels reason `Layout_test` already
holds Code 128 to:

| Symbol | modules + quiet zone | 4 px | 5 px |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Version 1 | 21 + 8 = 29 | 116 px ✅ | 145 px ❌ by 1 px |
| Version 2 | 25 + 8 = 33 | 132 px ✅ | 165 px ❌ |
| Version 3 | 29 + 8 = 37 | 148 px ❌ | ❌ |

**A five-pixel module misses by one pixel row.** A 145 px square needs its
bottom edge at y ≥ 169, and 169 is where the id starts. That is worth writing
down rather than rounding away, because the temptation to reclaim three pixels
from the id row is exactly the kind of move that has clipped this app's text on
device twice. The id row's position is the product of measurement, not
arithmetic (README.md §"Fitting the id on a round screen" has the table), and
re-opening it to buy 25 % of X-dimension would mean re-measuring all of it.

So the module is **4 px = 0.504 mm**, and the choice is version 1 or version 2.

**Whether 0.504 mm scans is the one load-bearing thing I cannot answer.** I have
not found a citable minimum module size for QR, and I have not checked whether
ISO/IEC 18004 sets one — I would expect it to leave X-dimension to the
application the way ISO/IEC 15417 does for Code 128, but I have not read it and
am not going to assert it. What can be said is that 0.504 mm is four times the
X-dimension this app already ships for its worst-case Code 128 id, and that the
failure mode on this panel is far more likely to be specular glare off the front
polariser than resolution. It needs a camera pointed at it. See §Evidence.

### The cost the layout imposes: no name

Every viable square overlaps the caption band (y = 48..72). Version 2 at 4 px
sits at y = 34..165; version 1 at 4 px sits no lower than y = 130..165 and still
overlaps. Dropping to a 3 px module (87 px, 0.378 mm) would clear the caption and
would trade the number that decides scanning for a label, which is the wrong way
round.

**In QR mode the code's name is not shown.** The id text and the pager marks
stay, so the wearer still sees which id it is and which of six they are on. This
is a genuine loss and should be in the README, not discovered.

## The design questions

### One list or two?

**One.** A QR code takes a slot in the same six and cycles with the same L1/L2.

`Barcode::Code` already carries a `Format`, so this costs nothing in the
message and nothing in the model. A second list would double the configuration
surface — twelve more rows on a phone form that is already twelve — to express
something the existing list expresses for free. The muddier-model objection in
SYMBOLOGIES.md applies to a QR that carries a *different kind of value*; it does
not apply to a QR that carries the same id.

### Does the payload limit change?

**No, and this is the load-bearing decision.**

`kMaxIdLength` is 16. QR version 2 at level M holds 26 bytes in byte mode, so
every id the app accepts fits with ten bytes spare. Leave it there and the
following all stay exactly as they are: the field's `maxLength: 17` and its
`[ -~]{0,16}` pattern, the phone's validate-as-you-type property, `Code::id`,
the two-tier/split id text layout, and `Problem::BadValue`'s prompt — which stays
*true*, since no id the app accepts can be refused by the QR encoder.

Raising it is where the money goes. The message is the wall. A full `State` must
fit a 256-byte kernel pool block; today it is 188 bytes of state plus 12 of
`MessageBase` = 200. Holding six codes and lengthening the id:

| longest id | `sizeof(Code)` | codes that still fit 256 B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 16 (today) | 31 | **6**, at 200 bytes |
| 23 | 38 | 6, at 242 bytes |
| 31 | 46 | 5 |
| 47 | 62 | 3 |
| 63 | 78 | 3 |
| 79 | 94 | 2 |
| 127 | 142 | 1 |

A short URL is 40-70 characters. So "QR can hold a URL" cashes out as *"the
watch holds two or three codes instead of six"*, which is a direct attack on the
thing the README says the app is for — a family at a finish funnel. Escaping
that means not sending the state in one message: a second message type, or
re-reading the config in the GUI, either of which is a larger change than the
encoder.

And the payload limit is not only a message question. Sixteen characters is also
what the id row can *draw* — README.md has the measured table, and a 16-character
id already needs the 18 pt face and sometimes two lines. A 60-character URL has
no human-readable rendering on this panel at all, which quietly deletes the half
of a barcode that this app treats as a contract with the wearer.

**If the answer later needs to be "yes", it is a different app.** That is
SYMBOLOGIES.md's own suggestion and I think it is right — for payloads, not for
formats.

### How is the format chosen?

A per-code `fmtN` string field, alongside `idN` and `nameN`.

```json
{ "id": "fmt1", "type": "string", "label": "Code 1 format",
  "default": "Code128", "maxLength": 8,
  "pattern": "(?:[Cc][Oo][Dd][Ee]128|[Qq][Rr][Cc][Oo][Dd][Ee])?",
  "validationMessage": "Code128 or QRCode." }
```

Two words, `Code128` and `QRCode`, and the field is **pre-filled with
`Code128`**. That matters more than it looks: the SDK has no enum type, so the
phone renders this as a plain text box, and a pre-filled value is the only thing
on the form that tells a wearer what the vocabulary *is*. An empty box would
make them read the description to discover that `QRCode` is the other word.

**Matched without regard to case**, because the wearer is typing and being fussy
about capitals would be a spelling test rather than a safety check. The pattern
spells both cases out per letter -- the dialect has no inline `(?i)` flag -- so
the phone accepts exactly what the app does. `qr` alone is refused: it is the
obvious thing to type and was the spelling in an earlier draft, but two words for
one format is a wart, and refusing costs nothing because the screen says what to
use.

**A missing `fmtN` reads as the declared default, which is `Code128`.** That is
what makes an `input.json` written before this field existed keep working;
`parseFormat()` also maps an empty value to Code 128, for a file hand-edited to
clear it. Both get tests of their own (§Evidence, item 2).

`configFields` goes from 12 to 18, against the SDK's limit of 32.
`kFieldsPerCode` goes 2 → 3, and the `static_assert` in `AppConfigFields.cpp`
plus the CI `validate_app_config.py --check-bounds` run keep the manifest and
the binary's copy in agreement — that machinery already exists and needs no
change beyond the new rows.

**Rejected: inferring the format from the payload.** "A URL means QR" is
in-band signalling in the one field the app's safety story rests on, which is
the same reason SYMBOLOGIES.md rejects an `itf:` prefix; and with a
sixteen-character limit no URL fits anyway, so there is nothing to infer from.

**Rejected: a global setting.** The motivating wearer has a parkrun code that
must be Code 128 and a gym card that wants QR, at the same time.

**Accepted with a caveat: `stringField` is a plain text box.** SYMBOLOGIES.md is
right that a typed format word is a poor phone control and that the SDK has no
enum type. It is less bad here than it would be for a three-way choice: there are
two words, one of them is pre-filled, and the phone *can* fully enforce this one
because — unlike ITF's even-digit rule — the pattern needs no knowledge of the
sibling `idN` field. This is the one place the cross-field validation gap that
document warns about does not bite. Pre-filling the default is what makes a text
box tolerable as a two-way toggle: the wearer edits a word rather than
remembering one.

**An unrecognised `fmtN` refuses the code**, and gets a new
`Problem::BadFormat` with its own prompt. This is a judgement call and the
counter-argument is decent: `Service.cpp` already decides that a bad *name* is
"decoration: a bad one costs the label, never the code", and a bad format is
arguably the same — the value drawn would still be the wearer's own id, just in
the wrong symbology, so non-negotiable #1 does not bite. I still prefer refusing,
because "silently drew something other than what you asked for" is the failure
this app's documentation is most allergic to, and because the phone's pattern
makes the case unreachable except from a hand-edited file. **Decided: refuse.**

### Where does the version and EC level come from?

**Fixed: version 2, error-correction level M, byte mode.** Not configurable and
not auto-selected.

The reasoning is the same one that keeps Code 128's subset choice out of the
config: a wearer cannot see an EC level, cannot debug one, and has no useful
opinion about one. Fixing it also deletes most of the encoder — see below.

Why these values:

- **Byte mode always.** The app accepts all printable ASCII. Alphanumeric mode is
  denser but uppercase-only, and since the version is fixed a denser mode buys a
  smaller symbol that we would not draw. So there is **no mode selection at all**,
  which is a whole decision tree that does not get written or tested.
- **Version 2, not 1.** Both fit at a 4 px module (132 px vs 116 px), so the
  module size — the only number that decides whether a camera resolves it — is
  identical. Version 2 buys level M instead of level L.
- **Level M, not L.** At version 1 the choice is forced: V1-M holds 14 bytes in
  byte mode, which cannot carry a 16-character id, so version 1 means level L.
  V2-M holds 26. Level M corrects up to 8 of 44 codewords against level L's
  2 of 26 — roughly 18 % against 7 %. The predicted failure mode on this panel is
  glare washing out part of the symbol, which is exactly what error correction is
  for, so the more robust level is worth 16 px of screen.
- **Not version 3.** 37 modules with quiet zone is 148 px at 4 px, which does not
  fit above the id row.

Capacity arithmetic, so it can be checked: V2-M is 44 total codewords, 28 data,
16 EC, **one block**. Byte mode spends 4 bits on the mode indicator and 8 on the
character count (8 bits for versions 1-9), so (28 × 8 − 12) / 8 = 26 characters.

## Architecture

`Encoded.hpp` and `Symbology.hpp` both say in their own comments that they
handle linear symbologies only and that a matrix symbology fits neither. Those
comments are correct and should stay correct.

**QR gets its own intermediate form and its own widget. The linear path is not
touched.**

```
Encoded.hpp     unchanged.  A run of widths; still a complete description of
                any linear symbology and still the wrong shape for a grid.
Matrix.hpp      new.  A square bitset:
                  uint8_t modules[(25*25 + 7) / 8];  // 79 bytes
                  uint8_t size;                      // 0 when unset
Symbology.hpp   gains a second overload and a predicate:
                  bool encode(Format, const char*, Encoded&);   // linear
                  bool encode(Format, const char*, Matrix&);    // matrix
                  bool isMatrix(Format);
                Both switches keep having no default, so a new Format without
                an encoder is still a compiler warning in exactly one file.
Qr.hpp          new.  The encoder.
QrWidget        new.  Draws a Matrix.
MainView        shows one widget or the other; suppresses the caption for QR.
Service.cpp     adoptCode() probes through whichever overload isMatrix() picks.
```

Rejected alternatives, and why:

- **`Encoded` as a variant.** Puts a discriminant and a branch on the path that
  draws every parkrun barcode, to serve a format that path cannot draw. The
  requirement that the linear path not get slower or more conditional is enough
  on its own; so is the fact that `Encoded.hpp`'s central claim would stop being
  true.
- **One widget with two draw paths.** Same objection, one layer down, plus
  `BarcodeWidget` is a `CanvasWidget` and the QR one should not be — see below.

### Drawing it

**Not a bitmap, and not the canvas renderer.**

Not a bitmap for the reason `MainView::showBarcode()` records at length: the
23 × 35 ABGR2222 button indicators in this app blit corrupt on device, which is
why it has no on-screen button hints. Filled rectangles are the path known to
work here — the pager marks are `touchgfx::Box` fills for exactly that reason.

Not the canvas renderer either. `BarcodeWidget` is a `CanvasWidget` and gets away
with it because a Code 128 symbol is at most 115 quadrilaterals in a single
horizontal sweep. A 25 × 25 grid is a different shape of problem: up to 13 dark
runs per row over 25 rows, ~325 disjoint rectangles, against a
`CANVAS_BUFFER_SIZE` of 3600 bytes. I have not measured what that costs and do
not intend to find out on device.

So: a plain `touchgfx::Widget` subclass whose `draw()` walks each module row,
finds runs of dark modules, and issues one `fillRect` per run — 4 px tall, 4n px
wide, always on whole-pixel boundaries, so nothing anti-aliases and the four grey
levels never come into it. One `touchgfx::Box` behind it carries the white
backing, which *is* the quiet zone, exactly as `barcodeBackground` already does
for the bars. **To verify before building:** that `LCD8bpp_ABGR2222` in the SDK
exposes `fillRect` as expected.

## What it costs

Measured, not estimated: the same toolchain and the same container, building
HEAD in a `git worktree` against the working tree, both at version 0.4.0.

| | baseline | with QR | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Barcode_0.4.0.uapp` | 114,132 B | **117,812 B** | **+3,680 B, +3.2 %** |
| GUI `.text` | 80,388 | 83,244 | +2,856 |
| GUI `.data` | 2,612 | 2,744 | +132 |
| Service `.text` | 21,380 | 21,680 | +300 |
| Service `.data` | 964 | 1,204 | +240 |
| Message | 200 B | **200 B** | **0** -- `Code::format` already existed |
| RAM | | | 79 B of `Matrix`, plus a same-sized function map while encoding |
| Config fields | 12 | 18 | against the SDK's 32 |

**The first build was 5,720 bytes and the measurement is what found the reason.**
`Barcode::isDrawable()` answered "can this be drawn?" by encoding a probe, which
dragged Reed-Solomon, the eight mask patterns and the penalty scoring into the
*service* binary -- 2,340 bytes in a process that never draws anything. Giving
QR a `Qr::accepts()` and having `Qr::encode()` call the same function as its
first step -- so it is one rule and not two opinions -- took the service back to
+300 bytes and the package to +3,680.

Against SYMBOLOGIES.md's estimate of "a few kilobytes of code and tables": the
encoder itself is 2,856 bytes in the GUI, which is at the low end of that and
lower than a general QR encoder would be, for the reasons in §"Where does the
version and EC level come from?". This document's own earlier estimate of
"~2-3 KB" for the whole feature was optimistic: it counted the encoder and
forgot that the config table, the extra `Problem`, the widget and the new prompt
all cost something too.

## Evidence

SYMBOLOGIES.md's line that "there is still no oracle in the tree" is out of date
-- `a119f9c` added one -- and that changed the risk calculus for this feature
more than anything else in this document. Four things were built, and one still
cannot be.

**1. zint, module for module, across every mask. 144 symbols, all matching.**
`Tests/oracle/generate_qr.cpp` links `libzint` and commits the module grids as
data, the same shape as the Code 128 oracle. Eighteen payloads times eight
masks, diffed by `ZintQrOracle`.

The mask is **forced** rather than auto-chosen, and that is what makes the diff
mean something: every mask yields a decodable symbol, so which one an encoder
picks is judgement rather than correctness, and diffing an auto-chosen mask
would call a legal difference a bug. Forcing it tests the codewords, the error
correction, the placement, the function patterns and both format strings, eight
times over.

The corpus is restricted to payloads where a *single byte-mode segment* is the
optimal encoding, by a stated rule rather than by trial: this encoder always
emits one byte segment, while zint optimises across modes and will split
`a1234567` into a byte `a` and numeric `1234567`. That is a different, equally
legal encoding. The payloads it excludes -- notably the parkrun shapes, which
are pure alphanumerics -- are covered by (3) instead.

**2. An `input.json` with no format field is byte-identical to today.**
`Service.AFileWithNoFormatFieldIsStillEveryCodeAsCode128` and its neighbours,
plus `ParseFormat.AnAbsentOrEmptyValueMeansCode128`. The Code 128 zint diff and
round trip are untouched and still green. 129 host tests pass, up from 111.

**3. An independent decode, of this encoder's own output.** Matching zint proves
the grid; it does not prove the grid *means* the id, and two implementations can
share a misreading. There is no QR decoder in the tree and writing one would be
a second implementation of the hard part, so instead the grids are rendered and
read back with **zbar** -- offline, beside the oracle generator, the way
`generate.cpp` runs outside the test build. All 23 payloads decode to exactly
their input, including the parkrun shapes zint segments differently, at every
module size from 2 px upward. At 1 px a module, 10 of 23 decode; that is not a
size this app draws, and it is recorded because it is the floor.

**4. The framebuffer the app actually draws.** `Tests/README.md` names a
framebuffer capture as the evidence this app has never had. It now exists.
Driven headless under `Xvfb`, captured from the simulator window, and checked
four ways:

| | QR, `GYMWORLD12345678` | QR, `0123456789ABCDEF` | Code 128 | the new prompt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ink outside the lit circle | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| modules differing from the encoder | **0** of 625 | **0** of 625 | -- | -- |
| pixels not matching their module | **0** | **0** | -- | -- |
| quiet-zone pixels not light | **0** | **0** | -- | -- |
| decoded by zbar | **exact** | **exact** | -- | -- |

The masking matters more here than anywhere else in this app: the simulator
draws the full 240x240 square with no bezel, so round-panel clipping is
invisible in it, and that has reached the watch twice. The mask comes from
`BarcodeLayout::pixelIsLit`'s own model rather than from ImageMagick's `-fx`,
which yields a uniform image when the expression is malformed and makes every
measurement read zero and look like a pass.

**5. What still cannot be said.** None of the above is a camera photographing a
reflective LCD through a front polariser. A pixel-perfect capture decoded by
zbar is a much stronger claim than this app has ever made -- it is end to end
through the real renderer at the real geometry -- and it is still not that. The
predicted failure mode on this panel is specular glare, which no capture of the
framebuffer can show. **0.504 mm modules on this glass have not been scanned by
a phone**, and until they have, that is where this feature's risk lives.

## What was decided

Agreed 2026-08-28. QR is built as a per-code format for the existing
sixteen-character id:

- `Format::Qr = 1`; `Format::Code128` stays 0 and stays the default.
- Fixed at **version 2, level M, byte mode**, 4 px per module, a 132 x 132 px
  symbol including its 4-module quiet zone, at x = 54, y = 34..165.
- A `fmtN` field per code, `Code128` or `QRCode`, either case, defaulting to
  the literal `Code128`; a missing or emptied value means Code 128.
- `kMaxIdLength` unchanged at 16. The message does not grow.
- Its own `Matrix` type and its own `fillRect`-based widget; the linear path
  untouched.
- **No name caption in QR mode**, and an unrecognised `fmtN` refuses the code.
- Held to a zint diff, an independent decode, a no-`fmt` regression test, and a
  masked screenshot.

### The layout question, and why it went the way it did

The caption was the contested part, and the first draft of this document
under-priced it. Keeping the name is not merely a smaller module: every QR that
fits *below* the caption band caps the symbol at 97 px, which forces version 1,
and version 1 forces level L -- V1-M holds 14 bytes in byte mode and cannot
carry a sixteen-character id. Moving the caption up four pixels opens a 101 px
band and recovers version 2 at a 3 px module, but not the module size. The three
options, measured:

| | symbol | module | error correction | capacity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| caption unmoved, V1-L @ 3 px | 87 px | 0.378 mm | 2 of 26 cw, ~7 % | 17 B |
| caption up 4 px, V2-M @ 3 px | 99 px | 0.378 mm | 8 of 44 cw, ~18 % | 26 B |
| **no caption, V2-M @ 4 px** | **132 px** | **0.504 mm** | **~18 %** | **26 B** |

The last was chosen: the module size is the only number that decides whether a
camera resolves the symbol, and 33 % of it is worth more than a label the pager
marks and the id text partly substitute for.

**One number this document nearly asserted and should not have.** An earlier
draft was going to reassure the reader that 0.504 mm is comparable to an Apple
Wallet QR on an Apple Watch. Worked through, it is not: an Apple Watch is about
40 mm across against this panel's 30.24 mm, and a Wallet pass QR is plausibly
around 0.9 mm per module. This panel cannot match it and there is no point
pretending otherwise. What can be said is that 0.504 mm is roughly four times
this app's worst-case Code 128 X-dimension. Whether it scans is item 5 of
Evidence and nothing in this document settles it.

### The case for not building it

The app's headline use is parkrun and the funnel laser cannot read QR, so if
every event scanned with the Opticon this would be a well-engineered answer to a
question nobody asked -- non-negotiable #5's outcome. What carries it is that
the camera route is in routine use and that watch-worn parkrun QR codes already
exist on other platforms. That is the justification; if it turns out to be
wrong, the format is one enum value and one header to remove.
